Part I

Overview & scope

What the package does, why the demonstration core cannot rotate, and how the fork reuses the validated physics without touching it. The immutable _engine kernel is never modified, so met-water-nucleation --validate stays green.

§1What this package is

storm_dynamics adds the physics a deep-convective storm needs in order to rotate, and — under a curved hodograph with surface friction and an evaporative cold pool — to spin up near-surface vertical vorticity, the proxy for tornadogenesis. It is a fork of the dynamical core of meteorological_flow: only the momentum/dynamics are rewritten; everything else is imported and used as a physics library.

Scope — be honest This is idealised simulation, not operational forecasting. There is no data assimilation, no real-event initial or boundary condition, and no observational verification. The deliverable is a rotational dynamical core validated against the classical idealised-supercell results (Klemp & Wilhelmson 1978; Weisman & Klemp 1982; Rotunno & Klemp 1985). Nothing here forecasts a real tornado, storm, or event, and the vortex is under-resolved at demonstration resolution.

The core produces the diagnostics that prove a storm rotated: the 3-D vorticity, the vertical vorticity ζ (mesocyclone / tornado indicator), updraft helicity, storm-relative helicity (SRH), and column trackers for the mid-level mesocyclone and the near-surface ζ.

§2Why the demonstration core can't rotate

The meteorological_flow storm makes three choices that are correct for a demonstration of nucleation-coupled convection but fatal to rotation:

  1. Momentum advection is switched off (a documented v1 simplification). With no (u·∇)u, there is no tilting of horizontal (shear) vorticity into the vertical and no stretching of vertical vorticity by the updraft — the two terms that make a mesocyclone.
  2. Rayleigh drag (gamma_damp) linearly relaxes the velocity every step — it would damp the very rotation we want.
  3. A hard velocity clip at ±120 m/s caps peak winds unphysically.

storm_dynamics fixes all three (§4, §6).

Part II

The rotational core

The seven pieces the fork adds, in the order they were built — each verified before the next. Item 1 (momentum advection) is the enabling term: without tilting and stretching there is no rotation.

§4Conservative momentum advection item 1

Flux/divergence form on each staggered control volume, with a 2nd-order MUSCL (minmod) reconstruction in the spirit of advect_center_massflux:

∂u_i/∂t = − ∂/∂x_j ( u_j u_i ) (flux / divergence form)

Because the transporting velocity is the projection's discretely divergence-free field, the flux form equals the advective form u_j ∂u_i/∂x_j while telescoping to the boundary flux — so the domain-integrated momentum of each component is conserved (to the boundary flux) at any intensity, with no velocity clip. Horizontal neighbours wrap (periodic laterals); the vertical is bounded (w=0 at the ground, outflow/damping at the top).

Verified. A spatially uniform flow gives exactly zero tendency (machine 0); the domain-integrated momentum tendency telescopes to ~1e−17 (machine precision) for all three components, on uniform and vertically stretched grids.

§5f-plane Coriolis item 2

A constant Coriolis parameter f = 2Ω sin(φ) for a Tornado-Alley latitude (~36 N):

du/dt = + f v′ dv/dt = − f u′ f(36°N) ≈ 8.6×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹

The force is applied to the perturbation wind (u′,v′) = (u−u₀, v−v₀), treating the environmental hodograph as a steady (geostrophically balanced) base state — the standard idealised-model choice (there is no imposed synoptic pressure-gradient to balance the full base wind). Coriolis is weak at storm scale over the ~1–2 h integration but is included for completeness and hemispheric sign.

§6LES subgrid closure item 3

The demonstration Rayleigh drag + velocity clip are replaced by a Smagorinsky eddy-viscosity closure, so the subgrid dissipation is set by the resolved strain rate:

K_m = (C_s Δ)² |S| · f_stab(Ri) |S| = √(2 S_ij S_ij) Δ = (dx dy dz)^(1/3) K_h = K_m / Pr_t f_stab = √( max(0, 1 − Ri/Ri_c) ) (Lilly stability correction)

Momentum feels d u_i/dt = ∂/∂x_j ( K_m ∂u_i/∂x_j ) and scalars feel K_h. Momentum diffusion is applied to the perturbation, so the environmental hodograph is not mixed away. The only remaining velocity bound is an extreme numerical guard (v_guard, default 150 m/s), documented as a safety rail that should never bite in a resolved run — not a physical cap.

les_model: smagorinsky (implemented) · none (background viscosity only) · tke15 (prognostic Deardorff TKE-1.5 — documented future work, raises NotImplementedError).

§7Surface bulk-drag item 4

Surface friction is essential to low-level rotation: it retards the near-ground wind, tilting the frictionally generated horizontal vorticity and driving the convergent corner flow that intensifies a near-surface vortex. A bulk drag law retards the lowest model level:

τ = ρ C_d |V| V du/dt|₀ = − C_d |V| u / dz₀ |V| = max( √(u²+v²), U_min )

C_d ~ 0.01–0.02 over land. Only the lowest cell is retarded (implicitly, so a strong low-level wind at coarse dz₀ cannot overshoot); the LES carries the momentum flux upward.

§8Curved hodograph & SRH item 5

The WK sounding's thermodynamics are reused unchanged; only the winds are replaced. A quarter-circle hodograph turns the wind through the lowest z_turn (veering with height), then holds constant — the curvature is the source of storm-relative helicity that selects the right-moving supercell and feeds low-level rotation. Storm-relative helicity (Davies-Jones):

SRH = ∫₀ʰ [ (v−c_y) ∂u/∂z − (u−c_x) ∂v/∂z ] dz (right-mover motion c from Bunkers)
HodographSRH₀₋₁SRH₀₋₃shear₀₋₆role
unidirectional (straight)~37~130~22 m/sM1 storm splitting
quarter-circle (curved)~146~470~34 m/sM2 low-level rotation

Both are positive (right-mover); the curved hodograph adds large positive SRH. Units m²/s².

§9Cold pool & nucleation kernel item 6

The reused MicrophysicsCoupler supplies growth/conversion, latent heat, sedimentation, and the evaporative cold pool — the baroclinic vorticity source at the heart of low-level tornadogenesis. Optionally (off by default), the validated 2nd-order nucleation kernel rate J is evaluated on the post-transport state each step and fed to the microphysics as the embryo source (eq39 pathway), exactly as meteorological_flow does:

build_storm_config(couple_nucleation=True, nucleation_method="lookup")
# or the example flag:  --kernel-nucleation

Building the lookup table is slow, so it is opt-in; the CCN/IN activation already drives the cold pool. The report records kernel_coupled.

§10Rotation diagnostics item 7

The numbers that prove rotation, from storm_dynamics.rotation:

ζ = ∂v/∂x − ∂u/∂y (vertical vorticity: mesocyclone / tornado indicator) UH = ∫ w ζ dz over 2–5 km (w>0, ζ>0) (updraft helicity: rotation-track measure) (ξ, η, ζ) = ∇ × u (full 3-D vorticity)

Trackers follow the mid-level mesocyclone (max |ζ| at ~3–6 km) and the near-surface ζ (max at ~0–1 km — the tornadogenesis proxy) over time; SRH and bulk shear come from the hodograph. storm_dynamics.plotting renders these as maps (the split couplet) and time series.

Part III

Running & milestones

Install, configure, and reproduce the two milestones — the rotating supercell (M1) and the low-level rotation proxy (M2) — with the diagnostics that prove the result, plus the honest boundary of what the numbers mean.

§11Install & quick start

The repository is self-contained (numpy / scipy / matplotlib). Run from src on the path:

# M1 - rotating supercell (storm splitting); --plots writes the rotation figures
PYTHONPATH=src python examples/supercell_tornadogenesis.py --scenario supercell --plots

# M2 - low-level rotation (curved hodograph + drag + cold pool)
PYTHONPATH=src python examples/supercell_tornadogenesis.py --scenario tornadogenesis \
    --nx 40 --ny 40 --nz 48 --duration 3600 --plots

# optional: couple the validated nucleation kernel (eq39 embryo source; slower)
PYTHONPATH=src python examples/supercell_tornadogenesis.py --scenario supercell --kernel-nucleation

# compute backend: --device cpu (default) | gpu (NVIDIA + CuPy) | auto
python examples/supercell_tornadogenesis.py --scenario supercell --device auto

# tests
PYTHONPATH=src python -m pytest tests/test_storm_dynamics.py tests/test_storm_milestones.py -q
CPU or GPU. The core is backend-agnostic (every hot-loop module uses grid.xp), so it runs on CPU (NumPy) or GPU (CuPy) with identical results (regression-tested). cpu is the default; gpu requires an NVIDIA GPU + CuPy and fails loudly if absent; auto falls back to CPU. At demonstration grid sizes (≤64k cells) the direct pressure solve stays on the host and CPU is usually faster; GPU pays off at larger grids (CG on the GPU).

Declarative configs load via the Python API:

from storm_dynamics.config import storm_config_from_yaml
from storm_dynamics.core import StormSimulation
sim = StormSimulation(storm_config_from_yaml("configs/storm_supercell.yaml"))
report = sim.run()
print(report["rotation"])   # zeta, updraft helicity, SRH, ...

§13M1 — rotating supercell

Unidirectional shear over the WK sounding: a saturated warm bubble grows into a deep updraft that splits into left- and right-moving cells (cyclonic and anticyclonic vertical vorticity of comparable magnitude) with a mid-level mesocyclone — the Klemp-Wilhelmson / Weisman-Klemp storm-splitting reference.

supercell — 32×32×40, Δx≈1.25 km, 40 min

grid : 32 x 32 x 40 (dx=1250 m, dz=400 m) hodograph : unidirectional f = 8.572e-05 1/s (lat 36.0 N) SRH 0-1 / 0-3 km: 48 / 130 m^2/s^2 shear 0-1/0-6 km: 6.4 / 22.4 m/s -------------------------------------------------------------------- w_max / w_min : +19.6 / -6.8 m/s zeta max / min : +5.04e-02 / -4.96e-02 1/s (storm splitting = both signs) mid-level meso : 1.06e-02 1/s (peak 1.13e-02) updraft helicity: 231.4 m^2/s^2 (peak 341.4) -------------------------------------------------------------------- conservation : water rel-err -1.5e-02 | energy 2.1e-03 | mass |div| 1.6e-04

What this shows. A near-symmetric cyclonic/anticyclonic ζ couplet straddling the updraft is the split-supercell mesocyclone; |ζ| at mid-levels exceeds the ~5×10⁻³ s⁻¹ mesocyclone threshold, and updraft helicity >100 m²/s² is solidly supercellular. The regression test uses a faster 24×24×36 / 20-min run (~30 s) that still splits and forms a mesocyclone.

§14M2 — low-level rotation (tornadogenesis proxy)

Curved (quarter-circle) hodograph → strong storm-relative helicity, plus the surface drag law and the evaporative cold pool. Near-surface vertical vorticity develops on the cold-pool / forward-flank interface — the target of the project.

tornadogenesis — curved + drag vs. straight control (identical numerics)

CURVED+DRAG : SRH01=170 w_max=18 near-surface zeta peak=3.33e-03 final=3.29e-03 (sustained) STRAIGHT+NOD: SRH01=38 w_max=23 near-surface zeta peak=2.48e-03 final=1.37e-03 (decays) -------------------------------------------------------------------- ratio (curved / straight) final near-surface zeta = ~2.4x water rel-err = -7.3e-04 mass-continuity residual = 1.9e-04

What this shows. The curved hodograph + friction sustain near-surface ζ (~3.3×10⁻³ s⁻¹, roughly steady) while the straight / no-drag control peaks lower and decays — ~2.4× the low-level rotation of the control — with a physical updraft (w≈18 m/s) and unchanged conservation.

Stability — resolution matters A strong low-level-SRH environment on a coarse uniform grid over-amplifies the updraft (grid-scale w blow-up to ~110 m/s, above the parcel CAPE limit). The shipped M2 config fixes this by clustering vertical levels (z_stretch=1.05, dz₀≈100 m), moderating the hodograph (U_max=18, z_turn=2000) and using a slightly larger C_s=0.22. These are documented demonstration choices, not tuning of the physics.

§15Numerics & conservation

  • Core: anelastic (div(ρ₀u)=0), reference density ρ₀(z) from the sounding — appropriate for a 10–16 km column (Boussinesq is stretched past validity there).
  • Time stepping: explicit predictor + pressure projection, adaptive CFL that now includes the advected momentum and the peak LES viscosity.
  • Conservation: water/energy close to the parent solver's levels; the continuity residual comes from the projection (~1e−5 normalised), not from limiters. Water/energy are conserved up to the boundary flux through the damping top — the expected, documented non-conservation channel.
  • No velocity clip: the only bound is the documented v_guard extreme rail.
Anelastic residual. Use the ρ₀-weighted mass_continuity_residual_norm (≈1e−5). The plain div(u) is intentionally nonzero for the anelastic system (= −w ∂ρ₀/∂z / ρ₀).

§16What it can — and cannot — claim

Can

  • The rotational dynamics are present and correct in kind: tilting and stretching produce a mid-level mesocyclone and storm splitting from a sheared environment (M1), reproducing the classical behaviour.
  • Under a curved hodograph with surface friction and an evaporative cold pool, near-surface vertical vorticity develops and is sustained (M2) — the qualitative tornadogenesis pathway.
  • Conservation (water, energy, mass continuity) holds to the parent solver's levels.

Cannot

  • It does not forecast any real storm, tornado, or event. No assimilation, no observed initial state, no verification against observations.
  • It does not resolve the tornado vortex. At Δx ~ 1 km the near-surface ζ is a proxy; peak ζ, updraft helicity and wind magnitudes are indicative, not quantitative. Quantitative low-level rotation needs O(100 m) grids (M3) and is explicit future work.
  • Idealisations: f-plane, analytic sounding, single warm-bubble trigger, single-moment bulk microphysics, fixed Smagorinsky constant — the standard idealised-modelling choices, which bound what the numbers mean.
Non-goals (explicit) Data assimilation, real-event initial/boundary conditions, operational forecasting, and verification against observations are out of scope. The deliverable is an idealised rotating-storm dynamics simulator built on the repository's already-validated physics.
